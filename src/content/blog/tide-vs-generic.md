The brand vs. generic decision gets oversimplified in both directions. "Always buy generic" is wrong. So is "name brands are worth the premium." The reality is category-specific — and knowing which is which is worth about $600–$800/year for the average household.

## Where generic wins clearly

**OTC medications**: FDA regulations require generics to have the same active ingredients in the same dosage as brand equivalents. Kirkland ibuprofen and Advil are chemically identical. Generic cetirizine and Zyrtec are the same drug. The difference is 60–80% in price.

**Cleaning supplies**: Bleach is bleach. Dish soap contains largely the same surfactants across brands. The "premium" cleaning products are mostly premium marketing.

**Laundry detergent**: The Tide premium is almost entirely brand spend. Costco's Kirkland Ultra Clean was produced by Procter & Gamble (the same company that makes Tide) for years. Generic HE detergent cleans clothes. Period.

**Dairy**: Milk, butter, shredded cheese, sour cream — in most cases, the store brand comes from the same regional co-ops as national brands. Quality difference is essentially zero.

**Canned goods**: Canned tomatoes, beans, corn, broth. The commodity is the commodity.

**Pantry staples**: Flour, sugar, salt, baking powder, vegetable oil. There is no meaningful difference.

## Where brand sometimes matters

**Condiments and sauces**: Taste varies significantly and it's genuinely subjective. Generic ketchup has a different flavor profile than Heinz. Hot sauce formulations differ. If you have a strong preference, it's reasonable to pay for it.

**Baking chocolate**: There's a real difference in quality. For everyday baking it doesn't matter. For a recipe where chocolate is the point, it might.

**Yogurt**: The difference in culture quality between generic yogurt and something like Chobani or Fage is real. Whether it's worth the 50–100% premium is a personal call.

## The Tide question specifically

Tide is the most defensible laundry premium because its formula is genuinely effective, especially on stains. But:

- Kirkland Ultra Clean (Costco) performs nearly identically in independent testing
- Most generic HE detergents clean everyday loads perfectly well
- The Tide premium runs 50–70% over generic

Unless you have specific staining challenges, generic is fine for most loads.

## A simple framework

Ask three questions before paying a brand premium:

1. **Is the active ingredient identical?** (Medications, cleaning products — look it up once)
2. **Have I actually noticed the difference in a blind test?** (Not "I think it's better" — have you actually tested it?)
3. **Is the cost difference more than 30%?** (Below that, the convenience of not thinking about it is worth it)

For most categories, you'll answer no, no, and yes — and switch to generic without looking back.

The households saving $600+ per year on this aren't denying themselves anything. They just ran the experiment.
